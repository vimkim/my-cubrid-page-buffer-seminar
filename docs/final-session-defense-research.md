## Generations, WAL, and DWB

### Problem and property: G1–G3

A resident frame can change again while an earlier image is being submitted. The safety requirement is that one submitted image has internally consistent bytes and the matching WAL boundary, and that completion of that image does not erase the later resident write obligation. A teaching generation `G` names one captured image, not a CUBRID integer generation field.

The common path enters with the BCB mutex held and with its caller responsible for flush eligibility. It marks FLUSHING, clears the old DIRTY bit, copies/encrypts the page, captures the page LSA and `oldest_unflush_lsa`, clears the resident oldest bound, then releases the BCB mutex. The eligibility protocol excludes a competing WRITE owner during capture; the mutex alone is not a substitute for that caller obligation. The FLUSHING flag also prevents victim reuse while this asynchronous completion is outstanding. A later writer can produce `DIRTY=1, FLUSHING=1`. Successful completion clears FLUSHING alone, preserving the later DIRTY state. This last rationale is explicit in the comment on `pgbuf_bcb_mark_is_flushing()`; the broader throughput explanation is Inference. [C1](#pinned-primary-evidence)

The captured LSA is an upper recovery obligation for the captured image. The oldest unflushed LSA is a lower pending/redo bound, with a different job. For logged changes, the common path forces WAL through the captured page LSA before page submission. Reading a newer resident LSA later can cause unnecessary forcing; reading an older unrelated LSA can understate the obligation. Capturing bytes and metadata together avoids both mismatches. Changes `A → B → A` can leave the same payload but a newer page LSA and outstanding logged work; equality of one payload field proves neither equal complete images nor retired recovery obligations. [C1](#pinned-primary-evidence), [C2](#pinned-primary-evidence)

Flusher's I/O consumes `x=0`; writer changes both fields under its normal ownership to `(1,1)`; I/O then consumes `y=1`. | The possible submitted image `(0,1)` is neither completed state. This is a constructed model of overlapping access to mutable bytes, not a claim that a stock CUBRID syscall executes this schedule. A live mutable pointer needs writer exclusion or another snapshot protocol. |

### Alternatives, costs, and proof boundary

A coherent no-copy alternative excludes content writers through WAL and page I/O. It preserves a stable resident image while extending lock hold time by the log/data wait. The pinned PostgreSQL `FlushBuffer()` is a concrete contrasting design: a share-exclusive or exclusive content lock protects the page LSN and bytes during its WAL force and write. Its exact content-lock modes must be taught from this revision, not from older PostgreSQL descriptions. [P6](#pinned-primary-evidence)

A hypothetical versioned/copy-on-write image can shorten resident writer exclusion, but needs image lifetime, matching LSA publication, and completion tied to the correct version. DWB and full-page WAL images solve a different problem. PostgreSQL's official full-page-write documentation explains recovery from partial page writes at the cost of additional WAL volume; it is evidence for the alternative recovery technique, not for CUBRID behavior. [PostgreSQL full-page writes](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/runtime-config-wal.html#GUC-FULL-PAGE-WRITES)

Let `b` be page bytes and `F` simultaneous captured images. Capture performs O(b) copying or encryption work and requires O(Fb) transient image storage, in addition to DWB's separately owned slots. An ordinary DWB-protected page path writes an image to staging and home, with batch/synchronization overhead; deduplication and batching prevent treating this as an exact universal two-device-write counter. WAL wait, slot pressure, storage latency, mutex reacquisition, and post-flush queueing can dominate elapsed time. [C1](#pinned-primary-evidence), [C3](#pinned-primary-evidence)

DWB block flush writes and synchronizes the DWB file before writing home locations; affected home volumes are then synchronized by the flush thread/helper protocol. Recovery checks a home page for corruption, checks the corresponding staging image, and replaces a corrupted home page only with a valid DWB image. This requires valid retained staging data and effective storage synchronization. It does not protect against arbitrary loss of both copies or prove every crash outcome. [C3](#pinned-primary-evidence)

Ordinary post-submission errors restore old DIRTY when appropriate and the captured oldest bound. Early TDE/DWB-slot exits precede that common cleanup and retain the separate VS-12 proof obligation. Do not claim universal failure rollback from the happy-path mechanism. A controlled writer-between-copy-and-completion probe discriminates generation handling; crash points after WAL force, DWB acceptance, DWB synchronization, and partial home propagation discriminate durability claims. [Uncertainty registry](../unresolved-or-version-sensitive-findings.md)

### Defense

**Objection:** “Copying is redundant because the writer already has a latch.” **Bounded answer:** the copy is useful because that writer exclusion ends before slow submission/completion. Remove the copy and also extend writer exclusion, and the safety property can still hold at a different contention cost. Remove only the copy while retaining overlap, and the argument fails.

**Follow-up:** “If A was restored, why is G+1 dirty?” **Answer:** dirty tracks an outstanding write/recovery obligation, not a comparison of one logical value. Identify the exact captured bytes, LSA, and completion event before claiming the obligation disappeared.

## Four daemon roles, wakes, and lifetime

### Problem, mechanism, and responsibility: D1–D4

The four roles separate policy recomputation, creation of clean replacement supply, CPU completion/handoff, and file-I/O pacing. Each role has its own task/thread, looper, and waiter. Only page-flush selects and submits cold dirty page images. Maintenance changes quotas; post-flush processes accepted submissions; flush-control replaces token budgets. These are cooperating control loops, not interchangeable workers or a mandatory pipeline. DWB's two daemon roles belong to another subsystem. [C4](#pinned-primary-evidence)

Nominal pauses are maintenance 100 ms, page-flush configured timer or wake-only, post-flush 1/10/100 ms then wake-only, and control 50 ms. The generic loop executes its first task before pausing, subtracts execution time from the nominal period, and uses zero pause if the period was consumed. Zero timed wait returns true, so page-flush's `was_woken_up()` can force work without a literal external wake. None of these numbers bounds actual start, end, or data persistence. [C4](#pinned-primary-evidence), [C5](#pinned-primary-evidence)

`waiter::wakeup()` returns when RUNNING; it does not accumulate a future event. Work persists in queues/state, not the signal count. The page-flush task checks allocator pressure/hit ratio; post-flush drains `flushed_bcbs`. The waiter's internal predicate protects its waiter state, while the task's queue/shared-condition checks answer whether useful work exists. These predicates have different owners and are not interchangeable. POSIX likewise requires re-evaluating a condition's shared predicate after a wait returns; a condition return does not establish that work remains. [C5](#pinned-primary-evidence), [POSIX condition wait](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/7908799/xsh/pthread_cond_wait.html)

Daemon tasks are boot-gated while recovery establishes pool/log/DWB prerequisites. Gate enable does not wake every role. Shutdown destruction sets stop, wakes a sleeper, and joins before resources are finalized; a working task must return before join can finish. Stop is neither cancellation of I/O nor joined termination by itself. [C6](#pinned-primary-evidence)

### Removal and alternatives

| Change | Concrete argument | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move quota adjustment to foreground calls | Existing private-list assignment/release already call the helper; periodic attempts disappear. In a quiet period with no such calls, quotas can remain stale. | Policy responsiveness and shifted foreground CPU/lock cost. |
| Remove background page-flush but retain allocator waiting unchanged | All searched pages are dirty, allocator enqueues, remaining actors perform no generation flush. The waiter receives no clean supply from that absent role. | Progress failure under the constructed conditions. A synchronous foreground flush/retry redesign can remain correct, as stand-alone/recovery fallback demonstrates. |
| Remove post-flush role and complete locally | Successful submission relocks the BCB, clears FLUSHING and wakes flush waiters using the implemented common fallback. | Correctness preserved; CPU and mutex work stays on page-flush, and that particular direct-offer opportunity is lost. |
| Remove pacing waits and retain WAL/I/O order | Writers proceed after each OS write instead of waiting for token replenishment. | Changed burst/load behavior; no demonstrated WAL safety violation. |
| Replace shared queue/state with “wake means one durable job” | Producer calls wake while consumer is RUNNING. No future wake is latched. Consumer later pauses with no durable work record to revisit. | Lost work/progress in the hypothetical design. Queue storage alone also does not prove a bounded queue-empty-to-sleep latency in the actual design. |
| Finalize the pool immediately after setting stop | Working daemon is still in its task and accesses a BCB after pool storage is retired. | Lifetime safety failure. Joining before reclamation supplies the required lifetime boundary. |

A hypothetical unified worker can fulfill all four responsibilities, but a long WAL/data wait then delays quota/completion/pacing work unless it uses a separate nonblocking schedule. Durable event counters or queue-aware sleep handshakes are coherent alternatives to wake hints, with additional synchronized state and ownership rules. They are not paths claimed to exist here.

Cost variables are `T` thread shards, `L` LRU descriptors, `D` demotions, `K` scanned nodes, `C` candidates, `N` neighbor extent, `Q` queued completions, and `W` waiters. Quota work is O(T+L+D); candidate work is O(T+L+K+C log C+C·N) when sorting/neighbors are enabled; post-flush is O(Q+stale allocator waiters+flush waiters), and token arithmetic is O(1) plus broadcasts. Q is work actually consumed during a drain; queue capacity does not bound a drain continually refilled by producers. Daemon existence, periodic attempts, or wake calls alone prove neither fairness nor bounded completion. Record task start/end, shared queue size, observed waiter state, wake/timeout classification, and I/O blocking to discriminate a scheduling stall from lack of work. [C4–C6](#pinned-primary-evidence)

**Defense question:** “Can the four threads be reduced to one without corruption?” **Answer:** potentially, if ownership, WAL, completion, and lifetime boundaries remain. The challenge is progress and contention, especially allowing I/O to delay the other responsibilities; source does not establish a throughput ranking.

## Candidate identity, protection gaps, and receiving claims

### Problem and mechanism: H1–H5

Candidate collection saves `BCB*` and VPID under one LRU lock, releases the list, optionally sorts, then acquires each BCB mutex. The BCB object is pool-owned persistent storage, while its page identity has a shorter lifetime. VPID revalidation distinguishes a current candidate for A from the same slot now holding B. It does not authorize reuse merely because A is still present. The later protected checks inspect DIRTY, FLUSHING, current ownership/waiters, victim zone, and WAL eligibility. [C7](#pinned-primary-evidence)

Safety guards forbid outstanding owners, pending images, and dirty retirement. LRU3/hot/private-quota choices retain valuable pages; they are policy preferences below safety. A promoted but clean and unowned BCB could theoretically be safely reused under a different replacement policy, whereas a cold pinned BCB cannot. The pinned `pgbuf_is_bcb_fixed_by_any()` conservatively includes queued waiters, and an unlocked precheck also inspects latch mode. [C7](#pinned-primary-evidence), [C10](#pinned-primary-evidence)

After successful submission, page-flush may enqueue a BCB pointer only when post-flush exists, allocators wait, and queue production succeeds. Otherwise it completes locally. The still-set FLUSHING flag prevents normal victim reuse while queued. Post-flush locks the BCB, rejects unsuitable current state or policy, may offer a direct victim, then always finishes old FLUSHING and wakes flush waiters. Enqueue is publication of completion work, not allocation or a fresh write. [C8](#pinned-primary-evidence)

Direct assignment locks the waiter entry, verifies it remains allocation-suspended, changes flags, publishes its slot, and wakes under the waiter lock. A new fix may invalidate the offer. The receiver atomically takes its slot, locks the BCB, rejects invalidated/currently ineligible state, removes list membership, and the allocation path separately victimizes the BCB and removes its old hash identity. Offer, receiving claim, and identity retirement are distinct boundaries. [C9](#pinned-primary-evidence), [C10](#pinned-primary-evidence)

### Removal schedules and alternatives

1. **Remove only saved VPID revalidation:** collect slot X for A, release protection, another actor reuses X for B, then X becomes dirty/cold. The stale candidate path flushes B while believing its selection concerned A. This is wrong selection attribution; with all current flush guards and the common path retained, it does not by itself prove corrupt bytes. Save both pointer and identity to preserve the selected-page premise.
2. **Remove only current eligibility after a gap:** observe A clean/unowned, release protection, reader fixes A, then reuse on identity equality alone. The reader's bytes can be retired while ownership remains. Same identity cannot replace a protected ownership/dirty-state check.
3. **Remove receiving revalidation:** offer A, another caller fixes/re-dirties A and releases, receiver uses stale offer. The new DIRTY obligation remains even if `fcnt` returned to zero. The invalidated-offer/current-state path must reject.
4. **Remove queue-full local completion:** submission succeeds but publication fails; FLUSHING remains with no queued owner to complete it. Later flush/reuse progress can be blocked. The implemented fallback closes this resource-pressure boundary.

A valid alternative holds exclusive candidate protection from selection through reuse, avoiding a protection gap but extending lock ownership across selection/I/O and conflicting with normal BCB→LRU acquisition. Ordinary list scanning uses BCB try-lock while holding LRU precisely to avoid waiting through the opposite lock order. Another coherent alternative claims a versioned identity token, but must still prove ownership exclusion, content stability, completion, and reclamation. A token alone is insufficient. [C10](#pinned-primary-evidence)

The selected-list victim walk inspects at most `min(Z3,1000)` BCB nodes, with up to `D` prior zone demotions outside that limit. CAS/try-lock failures and different list selections add retry work. A candidate-pool pass can span all LRUs and optionally sort C entries. These are structural CPU bounds for specified operations, not full allocator latency bounds. Post-flush rejection causes no new data write; cleanup still completes the old submission. [C7](#pinned-primary-evidence), [C8](#pinned-primary-evidence), [C10](#pinned-primary-evidence)

**Defense question:** “Why not delete every recheck when the saved identity matches?” **Answer:** identity says what page this slot currently represents. Ownership, dirty generations, waiters, retention policy, and offer validity can change without identity changing. Record before/after VPID, flags, fix/waiter state, zone/quota and rejection reason to identify the failed premise.

## Maintenance, accepted epochs, and pacing

### Problem and mechanism: P1–P5

Quotas and zones distribute replacement retention according to coarse observed private/shared activity. Eligible final unfixes contribute activity; the per-BCB `hit_age` gate normally contributes at most one hit per accepted `adjust_age` epoch. Adjustment consumes/reset samples, normalizes rate, smooths private activity and the private ratio over ten seconds, caps quotas, sets zone thresholds, demotes excess zones, and publishes victim-bearing lists. Private membership is a replacement domain, not transaction ownership. [C11](#pinned-primary-evidence), [Canonical private-domain evidence](../reference/private-lru-domain-hit-age-and-unfix-placement.md)

An attempt returns when quota is disabled, adjustment appears active, less than 1 ms elapsed, or both final-unfix activity is below B/4 and less than 500 ms elapsed. Only an accepted pass advances `adjust_age`; it can recompute the same numerical quota. Equality of quota values does not prove that samples were unconsumed or no epoch completed. The executable gate has no five-minute branch despite its comment. VS-21 also keeps hit-count synchronization, same-epoch BCB reuse, and signed wrap open; approximate policy inputs never authorize unsafe reuse. [C11](#pinned-primary-evidence), [Uncertainty registry](../unresolved-or-version-sensitive-findings.md)

File writers call compensation after the OS write completes. Non-log callers consume available tokens and condition-wait on deficit for at most ten return/retry iterations. A condition wait has no timed bound here; ten iterations does not mean at most 500 ms. LOG_CS_OWN callers account/consume but return on deficit, avoiding a pacing wait while holding the log critical section. Refill sets `tokens = generated`, replacing unused credit; it does not accumulate the remainder despite the comment. [C12](#pinned-primary-evidence)

### Removal, alternatives, and costs

| Removal or replacement | Same starting state and changed outcome | Bounded conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Static private quotas; retain all eligibility checks | Domain A becomes inactive and B becomes active. A's retained quota does not follow sampled use; B sees increased misses/eviction pressure in the illustrative workload. | Policy quality can change, not a proved safety defect or universal slowdown. |
| No per-BCB epoch gate; retain final-unfix sampling | One repeatedly released BCB contributes 100 samples while 100 other BCBs each contribute one; previously the first contributed one. | The measured signal changes from coarse distinct-BCB activity toward repeated release frequency. Which predicts useful retention better is a workload question. |
| Suppress epoch advance whenever quota numbers stay equal | Samples are reset and quotas remain 64; BCB hit_age still equals the old epoch. Its later eligible unfix contributes no fresh sample. | “Same quota” is not a valid completion condition for the sampling protocol. |
| Accumulate instead of replace token credit | Ten idle intervals each generate 20 tokens. A later burst sees 200 stored credits instead of one interval's 20. | Different burst policy. WAL/data ordering can remain intact. |
| Remove post-write pacing | First write is already complete in either case; caller immediately proceeds toward later work without token waiting. | Shifted workload/burst/foreground-latency tradeoff, not permission for the first write. |

A coherent fixed-quota scheme avoids periodic sampling and O(T+L+D) adjustment work but needs configured capacity partitions and may strand retention in inactive domains. Exact access accounting adds shared atomic/lock traffic; an epoch sampler reduces repeated-event influence while sacrificing access-frequency precision. A hypothetical pre-write limiter bounds admission before I/O, with explicit burst capacity and critical-section exemption rules. The existing post-write limiter cannot be presented as that design. None is an implemented alternative claimed here. [C11](#pinned-primary-evidence), [C12](#pinned-primary-evidence)

VS-20 is a directly checked source anomaly: each maintenance loop initializes index to its saved start and immediately tests `index != saved_start`, so neither body is entered. The initial budget 5 is not an assignment-per-call hard cap even in a repaired version, because the helper can assign multiple scanned BCBs. This rules out that pinned backup's body as a current producer; other producers still exist. Runtime allocator starvation, operational frequency and a supported variant's behavior require separate observations. [C13](#pinned-primary-evidence), [Uncertainty registry](../unresolved-or-version-sensitive-findings.md)

**Defense question:** “Could stale quotas corrupt pages?” **Answer:** quotas bias which currently safe pages are preferred and how zones are maintained. Correctness still requires the protected eligibility path. Correlate epoch acceptance/sample resets, quotas/zones, candidate rejections, producer-specific assignments and allocator waits to test a policy or progress hypothesis; do not infer it from one quota gauge.

## PostgreSQL private pins, shared state, and clock

### Problem and mechanism: K1–K5

One backend may nest several uses of the same shared frame. Private refcount records its nested debt; shared refcount ordinarily records one contribution while that backend's private count is positive. The first backend pin changes shared state and usage credit; a nested pin changes only its private count/resource ownership. The final private unpin decrements shared refcount. This lowers shared metadata traffic without releasing the frame while private use remains. It does not grant content access. [P1](#pinned-primary-evidence)

Pins preserve frame identity; content locks coordinate concurrent page interpretation/mutation. Credit is a bounded retention preference, saturating at 5 for default first pins. A strategy first pin raises zero credit to one but does not reduce already higher credit. Private nesting, shared pin count, usage credit, and content-lock state answer four different questions. The pinned implementation packs these into 64-bit state with atomic/CAS operations; older descriptions of 32-bit descriptors, header spinlocks, or shared-lock flushing cannot establish this revision's mechanics. [P1–P3](#pinned-primary-evidence)

Clock atomically advances a shared hand. A pinned visit skips; an unpinned positive-credit visit CAS-decrements then advances; an unpinned zero-credit visit CAS-increments shared refcount and tracks the new private pin. A decrement from 1 to 0 does not claim the frame on that visit. CAS failure reloads current expected state before deciding again. Observed zero is not the pin event. Concurrent hand advancement can produce apparent out-of-order processing. [P2](#pinned-primary-evidence)

### Removal and alternatives

| Exactly removed | Schedule | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared contribution; retain backend-private pin bookkeeping | A holds private pin. Replacement sees shared zero and claims/retires the frame. | Identity protection fails. A safe redesign must make private owners discoverable to replacement or impose other exclusion. |
| Content protection; retain pins | Two owners pin the same page. One compacts tuples while another reads a changing layout. | Pinning alone excludes reuse, not inconsistent content access. |
| Usage credit; retain pin checks and protected reuse | Previously hot page has shared zero when the hand reaches it; it can be claimed immediately. | Safety preserved, replacement preference changed. |
| CAS claim; retain zero-credit/zero-refcount observation | A reads zero; B pins; A unconditionally writes its stale incremented state. | The ownership update can overwrite or ignore intervening state. Atomic claim with expected-state revalidation prevents this schedule. |
| Credit saturation; retain increment/decrement policy | Hot page accumulates m credits; once cold, m successful decrement visits precede a later claim. | Aging work grows with history and counter-width concerns; not a standalone correctness failure. |

An exact LRU alternative can keep an intrusive ordered list and move an entry on every observed access in O(1) pointer work. It still needs synchronized ordering/list ownership and safety rechecks, and adds link storage and shared writes on hits. “LRU is O(N) per hit” is not a valid comparison. CLOCK trades exact recency for bounded credit and deferred scan work; the pinned `BM_MAX_USAGE_COUNT` comment explicitly calls this an accuracy/speed tradeoff. CLOCK-Pro and 2Q are primary-literature alternatives that retain limited replacement history to handle weak locality; they do not establish either pinned engine's active implementation or measured benefit. [P3](#pinned-primary-evidence), [CLOCK-Pro paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/2005-usenix-annual-technical-conference/clock-pro-effective-improvement-clock-replacement), [2Q paper](https://www.vldb.org/conf/1994/P439.PDF)

With `B` descriptors, maximum credit `U`, one selector, stable pin eligibility, at least one unpinned frame, no new credit, and no failed CAS, a loose bound is `(U+1)B` descriptor visits before a claim. This follows from at most U decrements per eligible frame plus the subsequent zero-credit visit. With U=5, six sweeps are a conservative isolated bound. The `trycounter=B` error test is different: pinned observations decrease it and successful decrements reset it. Under changing pins/credit, multiple hand movers, header waits or CAS interference, it gives no fixed total-scan or elapsed-time bound. Its error reports the encountered sequence, not proof that all frames were pinned at one simultaneous instant. [P2](#pinned-primary-evidence), [P3](#pinned-primary-evidence)

Let `V` be visits and `R` failed CAS attempts. Selection's structural work is O(V+R), plus header waiting; it still precedes dirty writeback and mapping retirement. Count visits, decrement successes, pin observations, CAS failures, credit replenishment and hand movement to discriminate too much aging work from owner contention.

**Defense question:** “Does bounded credit prove lock-free progress?” **Answer:** it bounds aging work only with the stated noninterference assumptions. A particular selector can lose CAS repeatedly, see moving ownership, or wait on a header. The source model is not a scheduler fairness proof.

## Rings, dirty writeback, and reuse

### Problem and mechanism: R1–R5

A large one-pass scan can populate the shared pool with pages unlikely to be reused, displacing a smaller working set. A backend-local strategy ring records a small set of shared Buffer IDs for repeated reuse. It owns no private page bytes and grants no pin reservation. Another backend, a second ring, or global clock can name/use the same frame; shared state determines eligibility. The pinned README explicitly motivates rings as scan-pressure control, while actual sizes/claims come from executable source. [P4](#pinned-primary-evidence)

One ring step advances its local slot, resolves the shared frame ID, rejects a pinned frame or credit above one, and CAS-pins a usable frame. An empty/rejected slot falls back to global selection and replaces that slot ID. Bulk-read starts with 256 KiB but at this revision can grow for configured I/O concurrency, subject to a pin-limit cap; every created ring is capped to B/8 descriptors. A fixed 256 KiB assertion misses the pinned expansion. [P4](#pinned-primary-evidence)

Dirty selection still needs a conditional share-exclusive content lock, WAL forcing, write I/O and later protected invalidation. The conditional lock avoids a possible deadlock with a backend that holds that content lock while waiting for other pages owned by the selector. For permanent, dirty, from-ring candidates whose page LSN needs WAL flushing, only BULKREAD may reject via `StrategyRejectBuffer()`. That invalidates the local slot ID, preventing repeated dirty-ring rejection; it does not erase shared frame identity or dirty data. BULKWRITE/VACUUM do not take this rejection. [P5](#pinned-primary-evidence)

### Removal schedules and alternatives

1. **Remove ring; retain global clock and pin/WAL/mapping checks:** scan more than B distinct cold pages between reuse of hot pages. The scan now competes through global replacement; displaced working-set pages can cause later misses. This demonstrates possible cache pollution, not corruption or a universal regression.
2. **Remove ring shared-refcount check:** rings A and B both contain frame X. A has already pinned X; B treats its local ID as ownership and reuses X. B violates the shared frame's identity protection. Distinct ring objects do not create distinct bytes.
3. **Remove only credit>1 rejection:** a frame used by other work becomes high-credit but unpinned; ring claims it on its next slot step. Pin/mapping safety can remain, while retention policy is weakened.
4. **Remove local slot invalidation on BULKREAD WAL rejection:** every slot refers to dirty, WAL-blocked permanent frames. Cycling can repeatedly reject the same ring entries instead of escaping to obtain replacements. Current invalidation supplies the escape state; it does not guarantee fast global supply.
5. **Remove final protected dirty/refcount recheck:** A claims X, writes it, unlocks content. B pins X, writes/logs a new change, dirties, unlocks and unpins. Shared count returns to A's one, but DIRTY stays set. A retires the frame using only its old snapshot and loses the new pending obligation. `InvalidateVictimBuffer()` rejects precisely this refcount-one-but-dirty outcome under mapping-partition and header protection. [P5](#pinned-primary-evidence)

A coherent scan partition with its own frame memory isolates capacity but duplicates identity/coherence work and can strand memory when idle. A local ring preserves shared lookup/reuse and admits interference, avoiding a private-copy claim. A globally scan-resistant policy such as 2Q is another alternative without per-scan hints, at the cost of extra queues/history/state. No source here ranks these choices for the user's workload. [P4](#pinned-primary-evidence), [2Q paper](https://www.vldb.org/conf/1994/P439.PDF)

Ring lookup/splice is O(1) local work plus CAS retries; memory is O(r) IDs for r ring slots. Ring misses add global scan work; dirty reuse adds WAL/data/content-lock waits and mapping retirement. r limits retained local references, not exclusive reserved frames or all I/O issued by a scan. Two overlapping rings can have fewer than their summed slot counts of distinct frames. [P4–P6](#pinned-primary-evidence)

### Backend, background writer, and checkpoint boundaries

The backend victim path performs work needed for its current request. Background writer scans ahead to create likely reusable clean supply, bounded by its estimate/scan/max-pages policy, and calls `SyncOneBuffer(..., true, ...)` to skip recently used/pinned frames. Checkpoint first marks the permanent dirty buffers in its checkpoint scope, then coordinates their writes; its `SyncOneBuffer(..., false, ...)` does not share the background writer's skip preference. Flushes by other actors can satisfy a marked checkpoint write. Full durable checkpoint completion includes synchronization and log-record work outside simple candidate selection. [P7](#pinned-primary-evidence)

**Defense question:** “If the other backend released its pin, why must A retry?” **Answer:** releasing ownership does not undo its dirty change. A must test both current sole pin and current cleanliness when retiring the old identity. Collect ring/global provenance, shared refcount, content-lock rejection, LSN/WAL need, dirty state after unlock, and invalidation outcome to distinguish retention rejection from writeback or reuse contention.

## Synthesis and diagnosis

### Complete argument: S1–S3

Use one interleaving rather than three independent “flush succeeded” statements: CUBRID captures G, writer creates G+1 while G is submitted, post-flush receives G's completion, allocator wants the frame, and the writer releases. G completion preserves G+1 DIRTY; post-flush declines direct assignment; unfix removes ownership but does not erase DIRTY; a later WAL-respecting generation flush is required before clean protected victimization can retire the identity. In PostgreSQL, a corresponding re-dirty after content unlock also defeats final invalidation even after the other pin returns to zero. The engines use different protected paths to preserve the same pending-update/ownership obligations. [C1](#pinned-primary-evidence), [C8–C10](#pinned-primary-evidence), [P5–P6](#pinned-primary-evidence)

For the proposed optimization “reuse immediately after successful image submission,” ask which completion was observed, which image it covered, whether another writer/owner intervened, whether the old identity is protected, and whether storage acceptance means synchronized persistence. An alternative implementation can serialize those actors instead, but its proof must include the changed lock lifetime and wait/deadlock costs.

The final defense is complete only when participants can name a still-preserved invariant, the failed assumption, a coherent alternative, and the observation that separates plausible causes. Static traces, source checks, presentation browser tests, and observed engine behavior are different evidence classes. Human comprehension, rehearsal, crash behavior, concurrency liveness and performance remain unmeasured here.
