1 / 6 · Problem and invariant

Defend each transition with its own evidence

For request Q in a full pool, identify the old page P, frame F, lifetime users, mutation protection, dirty generations and next actor. “Page written,” “daemon awake” and “candidate found” certify different events. None alone proves safe reuse.

End-to-end obligation: Keep the requested identity bound to a live protected frame; preserve P's required image/log history; prevent protected users from losing their frame; retire the old identity only after current reuse authorization; publish/load Q under its own protocol.

The exercise must distinguish a safety proof from a progress explanation. A safe rejection may leave Q waiting. A performance improvement can leave every invariant intact, while an apparently faster shortcut can erase one.

2 / 6 · Mechanism and evidence

Protection follows ownership across asynchronous boundaries

  1. Acquire identity/lifetime protection for use; content access follows its own rules.
  2. Mutation creates log and propagation obligations; release repays use debt only.
  3. Capture/submit an image under its WAL rule; image completion does not retire future mutations.
  4. Recheck current state when selecting, consuming, receiving or invalidating across a protection gap.
  5. Retire the old mapping and materialize the requested identity only through the protected acquisition protocol.

4 / 6 · Alternatives and costs

Alternative protocols need explicit receipts and costs

Teaching alternative: Transfer typed receipts for “image G accepted,” “G bookkeeping completed,” “current F reserved” and “old mapping retired.” Each receipt names its identity/generation/protected interval and cancellation rule. A reservation may exclude new users; an image receipt must never be promoted into lifetime ownership.

Continuous reservation spends concurrency and requires abandonment cleanup. Revalidation spends metadata reads, synchronization and possible retry. Foreground completion shifts CPU/I/O waiting to the requesting caller; background work shifts it to another actor and introduces queue/lifecycle dependencies.

Sum useful scan, image-copy, queue and state-transition work separately from lock, WAL, storage and scheduler delay. An upper bound on structural work under a quiet schedule is not a bound on concurrent end-to-end latency. Any throughput claim needs a controlled workload, storage configuration, pin durations and repeated measurements.

6 / 6 · Defense and falsifiability

D32 / D33 · Finish with a source-bounded defense

Allocator Q waits despite increasing flush-completion counters and a healthy post-flush heartbeat. Propose two causes that preserve correctness and one observation separating them.

Reveal the diagnostic defense

Completed pages may all have new dirty work, or clean pages may fail zone/private-quota policy. Both preserve safe reuse. A BCB-lock snapshot and reason-specific rejection counts separate them; heartbeat and flush totals do not. Also inspect live waiter state before asserting a missing wake.

Follow-up: Compare PostgreSQL: a rising write count with failed invalidation may likewise reflect re-dirty or extra pins. The matching idea is the responsibility boundary, not identical interfaces.

A change eliminates one protected recheck for speed. What must a defense contain before that claim is accepted?

Reveal the change defense

Name the removed interval and every actor allowed to change its predicates. Supply a replacement exclusion/version/lifetime proof, failure/cancellation cleanup and a counterexample search. Measure the intended cost with baseline and changed schedules; source reasoning cannot prove a speedup.
