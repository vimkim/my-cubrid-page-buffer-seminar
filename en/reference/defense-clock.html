1 / 6 · Problem and invariant

Four quantities prevent four different mistakes

A backend may use the same buffer repeatedly while another backend accesses its bytes. Replacement needs a shared indication of lifetime, while byte mutation needs content synchronization and policy needs a retention preference. Collapsing these quantities confuses ownership with popularity.

QuantityProperty
Private refcountNested use debt for this backend, including resource-owner bookkeeping.
Shared refcountBackend lifetime contributions: a nonzero count blocks replacement selection.
usage_countBounded reuse preference, not a lifetime right or exact recency timestamp.
Content lockPermitted concurrent byte operations while identity remains pinned.

Invariant: Keep a pin for identity lifetime and the required content protection for byte access. A pin alone does not serialize mutation; credit zero does not permit evicting a pinned frame.

2 / 6 · Mechanism and evidence

A decrement is a visit; a successful claim CAS is a pin

On a backend's first normal pin, the pinned source increases shared refcount and usage_count up to five. Same-backend nested pins increase private count only. A strategy pin raises zero credit to one and preserves a higher existing value. Repeated nested calls do not give unlimited shared credit.

The clock advances an atomic hand, skips nonzero refcount, decrements positive credit by CAS, and continues to a later visit. For unpinned zero-credit state it attempts a CAS adding its pin, then records ownership. A failed CAS retries against the changed state; an observation of zero is not a claim.

Verified mechanism / Implementation policy: bufmgr.c:3295–3386 · freelist.c:239–317 · packed state and maximum credit. This revision uses 64-bit packed state, including content-lock state; older spinlock summaries are not the executable authority.

3 / 6 · Removal counterfactual

Removing lifetime protection is a correctness change

Content protection cannot replace a pin

Constructed backend A uses H, credit zero (u=usage_count), between content-lock intervals. Remove only shared lifetime protection in the middle lane. Alternative counts every nested pin in shared metadata with matching releases.

    Implemented private/shared pins
    private=2; shared=1; u=0
    No shared lifetime pin
    private=2; shared=0; u=0
    Per-pin shared-count alternative
    private ledger=2; shared=2; u=0
    A / content access
    H identity retained; no content lock now

    Guard / assumption: A must retain a lifetime pin between byte accesses.

    Changed / outcome: Private nesting compresses one backend's shared contribution.

  2. 2. Clock visits H

    Implemented private/shared pins
    skip pinned H
    No shared lifetime pin
    CAS can claim H
    Per-pin shared-count alternative
    skip pinned H
    A / content access
    A still intends later access

    Guard / assumption: Selection excludes nonzero shared refcount; credit alone is not ownership.

    Changed / outcome: Removing lifetime state makes H falsely eligible; content locks are absent in this interval.

  3. 3. A releases one nested use

    Implemented private/shared pins
    private=1; shared=1
    No shared lifetime pin
    identity no longer protected
    Per-pin shared-count alternative
    private ledger=1; shared=1
    A / content access
    one outstanding use remains

4 / 6 · Alternatives and costs

Exact recency is a different synchronization budget

Teaching alternative: Maintain a shared doubly linked exact-LRU order and move a frame on each chosen access. A known-node splice is O(1) pointer work, but concurrent hits need list synchronization and metadata writes. Clock instead uses bounded credit updates and defers aging to allocation visits; it approximates retention and shares an atomic hand/state.

A FIFO or zero-credit clock can still respect pins/content/WAL rules while changing victim quality. Removing only credit is a policy change, not inherently corruption. Counting every nested pin in shared state also preserves lifetime if every release matches, but adds shared atomics and changes the meaning of the count.

For V visited descriptors and R failed CAS attempts, selection has O(V + R) state operations, excluding header-lock waits, content waits and writeback. Descriptor policy state is O(N) for N buffers; a backend adds private use state. Source does not tell us which alternative is faster under a given contention pattern.

CLOCK-Pro and 2Q study richer replacement history with extra policy state. They describe alternatives, not either pinned engine's active policy or a performance result for this workload.

5 / 6 · Concurrency and failure boundaries

A finite credit cap is not a concurrent progress theorem

Under a single allocator, stable pin membership, at least one permanently unpinned frame, no new credit and successful state operations, a credit cap U gives a loose bound of N(U+1) visits to find a zero-credit frame. This is a teaching bound on visits, not elapsed time. With U=5, a quiet static pool cannot retain positive credit indefinitely.

Concurrent backends can add credit, pin candidates, change the hand and interfere with CAS. The trycounter starts at N but resets on successful decrement; N is not a total scan cap. A sequence of pinned visits without intervening progress can instead report “no unpinned buffers available.” This does not prove permanent exhaustion across all future schedules.

atomic hand · retry/reset/error branches. Neither global allocator fairness nor a maximum latency is established.

6 / 6 · Defense and falsifiability

D29 · Does usage_count≤5 bound one allocation to six passes?

In a four-descriptor teaching pool, all frames are initially unpinned and credit≤5. Another backend repeatedly pins a candidate and renews its credit before the allocator's claim. Can the quiet-pool bound be reused unchanged?

Challenge, then reveal the bounded answer

No. The proof's no-new-credit and stable-pin premises fail. Saturation bounds the instantaneous counter, not the number of replenishments or CAS failures. State visits, successful decrements and successful claims must be counted separately.

Follow-up: “An exact LRU avoids this?” It still needs lifetime protection and a rule for concurrent usage. Replacing the policy does not discharge reuse safety or give scheduler fairness.

Discriminating observation: Collect hand tickets, observed pins/credit, successful decrement/claim CAS and interference retries. Hold workload and pin duration constant when comparing policies. Preserve Q18–Q20 and both existing fresh clock traces.
