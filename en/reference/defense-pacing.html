1 / 6 · Problem and invariant

Adapt retention separately from storage pacing

Private/shared domains retain different portions of the same pool. If workload activity shifts from A to B, frozen quotas can keep useful B pages under pressure while inactive A retains capacity. Quotas and zone thresholds are a retention policy; current fix/dirty checks remain the reuse safety boundary.

Policy objective: Reallocate retention using approximate recent activity. Progress intent: Publish candidate supply as zones change. Pacing intent: Delay future writer work after writes to moderate pressure. None proves fairness, a throughput ceiling or a maximum allocation wait.

Removing adaptation may worsen or improve a particular workload's hit rate; it need not corrupt a page. Treat a performance claim as a workload-dependent hypothesis.

Zones separate retained hot prefixes from cold candidate supply. Quota/threshold adjustment can demote excess retained entries toward LRU3; it does not grant permission to discard fixes or dirty bytes. A single undivided list with protected eligibility could remain correct, but must implement a different retention/admission preference.

2 / 6 · Mechanism and evidence

Accepted epochs and values answer different questions

A quota attempt can return because adjustment is disabled/already active, elapsed time is below 1 ms, or activity is below threshold while elapsed time is below 500 ms. Accepted passes update time and increment adjust_age, collect/reset hits, smooth activity over a 10-second horizon, calculate quotas and adjust zones. Equal before/after quota values do not mean no pass occurred: sampling and the epoch can still advance.

File I/O performs OS writing before its compensation call on the selected branch. The control task computes an elapsed-interval budget and assigns it to the shared bucket; it does not add the old unused tokens. Ordinary deficit uses a condition wait with at most ten condition-return retries. Log-CS ownership bypasses deficit waiting.

Verified mechanism: page_buffer.c:14251–14511 · file_io.c:751–891 · post-write accounting.

An eligible final unfix normally contributes a per-BCB hit only when its hit_age differs from the accepted adjust_age. This limits repeated releases of one BCB within an epoch. Suppressing epoch advance just because quotas stayed equal would also suppress fresh samples. Pinned sampling gate; reuse/wrap qualifications stay with VS-21.

4 / 6 · Alternatives and costs

Choose an objective before replacing the policy

Teaching alternatives: Fixed quotas reduce sampling/update work and make the retention split predictable, but cannot react to a changed mix. Exact synchronized hit accounting can improve sampling fidelity at a per-hit synchronization cost. A classical accumulating bucket needs an explicit capacity cap to bound saved credit; a pre-write admission limiter changes where callers wait and must preserve WAL/log-holder progress.

For T counter shards, L LRUs and Z nodes moved by zone adjustment, a quota pass has O(T + L + Z) structural work plus list mutexes and publication retries. Metadata is O(L + T), separate from BCB storage. A token request uses shared mutex/condition state; retries are at most ten, but condition waits are untimed and mutex acquisition has no source-only duration bound.

Neither a nominal 50 ms refill nor ten retries proves an elapsed-time bound. Measure burst size, writer delay and log-CS bypass traffic before claiming a strict rate limit.

6 / 6 · Defense and falsifiability

D28 · Can unchanged quota and ten retries prove “nothing happened”?

A profiler samples quota=64 twice, adjust_age advances, and one writer remains asleep after its first condition wait. A reviewer calls the policy frozen and the wait bounded by ten periods.

Challenge, then reveal the bounded answer

An accepted epoch can reproduce quota 64 while consuming samples and updating time/zones. A retry increments only after condition wait returns. The first untimed wait can last arbitrarily long under adverse scheduling or absent notification, so ten is not ten periods.

Follow-up: “Should we repair the skipped backup immediately?” First attribute impact by producer under supported conditions. Source anomaly and measured progress impact are separate claims.

Discriminating observation: Record accepted epochs, input samples, resulting zones, assignments by producer, refill times and condition returns. Compare static/adaptive quotas with a controlled changing workload. Existing Q14–Q17 preserve the prior policy and pacing challenges.
