Curriculum and practice →

01

Foundations

  1. Lecture 1 — Module boundary and page journeyFollow a logical page from acquisition through mutation, propagation, and frame reuse. Each transition has an owner and an evidence boundary.
  2. Lecture 2 — Objects and independent state axesIdentity, resident storage, borrowed access, and ownership accounting describe different parts of the same page journey.

02

Acquisition and ownership

  1. Lecture 3 — Hit and miss convergenceHit and miss prepare access differently, then converge on identity checks and the caller's ownership contract.
  2. Lecture 4 — Fix debt and releaseEach successful fix adds a release obligation. Global and per-thread ledgers make nested ownership visible.
  3. Lecture 4A — Holder structure and lifetimeThe thread's anchor and its reusable holder entries connect repeated BCB access to ownership accounting and lookup cost.

03

Mutation and durability

  1. Lecture 5 — Caller correctness and logged mutationA successful fix grants access. The caller still owns logical locking, validation, logging, dirty publication, and cleanup.
  2. Lecture 6 — Flush generations and WAL orderingA completed flush belongs to one copied image. Newer resident changes, checkpoint selection, and persistence require separate reasoning.
  3. Lecture 6A — Page-buffer daemon lifecycleFour background roles share a thread lifecycle but perform different work after recovery opens their gate.
  4. Lecture 6B — Page-flush and post-flush handoffAllocator pressure connects candidate selection, generation submission, protected rechecks, and allocator wakeup.
  5. Lecture 6C — Replacement maintenance and I/O pacingQuota maintenance and post-write token control shape future progress through separate state and timing policies.

04

Replacement

  1. Lecture 7 — Victim eligibility and frame reuseA missing page needs storage. Reuse requires a protected safety decision as well as a replacement preference.
  2. Lecture 12 — Replacement state and progressOne permanent BCB/frame pair moves through admission, cooling, victim checks, and rebinding to another identity.
  3. Lecture 12B — Private LRU indexes and selectionPersistent list descriptors, borrowed session indexes, BCB membership, and victim search have distinct lifetimes and rules.
  4. Lecture 12A — Dormant AOUT admission historyAOUT retains ghost admission metadata in the source, while pinned parameter tuning disables its operation.

05

Concurrency

  1. Lecture 9 — Latch queues and grant outcomesA pending request, a latch grant, and completed holder accounting are distinct events in the wait path.
  2. Lecture 10 — Promotion and page-state continuityDedicated promotion transforms existing ownership; its successful handoff and failure outcomes determine what the caller may retain.
  3. Lecture 11 — Ordered refix and revalidationOrdering breaks multi-page wait cycles. Releasing and reacquiring pages can also invalidate observations made before the gap.

06

Recovery and specialized behavior

  1. Lecture 13 — Log buffers and data-page redoLog storage and data-page residency have separate owners. Page LSA connects a recovered record to the skip-or-apply decision.
  2. Lecture 14 — Startup and shutdown dependenciesInitialization, recovery gates, and finalization must respect the last user of each resource.
  3. Lecture 15 — Specialized access contractsSimilar pointer types may carry different ownership, protection, permissions, and cleanup obligations.
  4. Lecture 16 — Failure cleanup and evidenceA source-visible return begins an investigation. Surviving state, retry behavior, and impact determine the supported conclusion.

07

Maintainer integration

  1. Lecture 8 — Change impact and verificationA change review connects interface behavior, state ownership, invariants, failure cleanup, caller impact, and verification.
  2. Lecture 17 — Maintainer synthesis and technical defenseA coherent page journey connects source traces, counterexamples, diagnosis, and a defensible change plan.

08

Cross-engine perspective

  1. Lecture 18 — Responsibilities across three buffer poolsCUBRID, PostgreSQL, and InnoDB solve shared safety problems through different ownership and cleanup mechanisms.
  2. Lecture 18A — Replacement policies across enginesAdmission, reuse history, victim selection, and dirty-page progress provide common questions for a source-based comparison.
