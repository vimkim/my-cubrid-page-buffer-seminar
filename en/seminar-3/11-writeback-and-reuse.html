From a dirty candidate to actual reuse

Selecting and pinning a dirty candidate does not finish its reuse. The backend may need conditional content protection, WAL and writeback before retiring the mapping. Other backends can pin or dirty the frame after content unlock. A background writer creates useful supply; checkpoint work has a separate durability obligation.

Final mapping retirement needs current sole pin and absence of newly dirty work under protection. Another backend’s unpin can return refcount to one while leaving DIRTY set. Writeback is an earlier event.

Q22 · Background cleaning versus checkpoint work

Constructed starting state: Unpinned dirty P has credit 3 and belongs to this checkpoint’s required work. Unpinned dirty Q has credit 0. A proposal skips checkpoint page processing because background writer is running.

Prediction: How do the two actors’ required outputs differ?

Discuss, then reveal

Background writer tries to produce clean near-future victims, using pin/credit eligibility and its scan budget; Q is a plausible eligible target. Checkpoint processing covers its selected checkpoint obligation, which includes P despite positive credit. Thread existence or cleaning Q does not discharge P’s checkpoint work. A foreground backend can also flush its selected dirty victim. Measure comparable workloads before claiming latency superiority.

CUBRID models competing domains more explicitly

CUBRID’s private replacement domains associate sessions with residency accounting and adaptive quota policy. Shared domains hold common policy membership. This is a richer model of capacity competition than PostgreSQL’s ordinary global Clock; PostgreSQL’s private refcounts track outstanding uses, and its strategy rings track a bulk operation’s preferred frame IDs.

In the compared PostgreSQL path, ordinary clients do not receive a CUBRID-style private-LRU residency quota. Reuse credit aggregates qualifying accesses without remembering which users supplied it. Rings add operation-specific scan control. Neither a pin count nor a ring gives general per-user capacity fairness.

Inference: CUBRID’s domain accounting may help a mixed workload; maintaining quotas, queues and list movement also adds work. A private domain is not an exclusive cache, and the same private list can serve multiple sessions. More policy state does not by itself prove a better hit ratio or throughput.

Separate domain migration from hot-zone promotion

Private → shared migration: on the eligible final-unfix path, CUBRID tests whether the caller’s private-list index differs from the BCB’s list, or whether that BCB is hot and old enough. The move inserts it into the shared list’s middle. This tests domain association, not a stored census of every distinct user. Two sessions assigned the same private list need not trigger the different-index branch.

Hot-zone boost: the ordinary unfix path keeps an LRU1 page in place, boosts LRU2 only when old enough, and boosts LRU3. This changes retention position; it is separate from private→shared migration and still does not authorize unsafe reuse.

PostgreSQL also promotes retention preference in a broader sense: qualifying normal pins add bounded credit, and credit above 1 makes a ring skip the frame. It does not promote a descriptor into a shared LRU. Compare what each policy remembers and costs, rather than treating the name “promotion” as a proof of superiority.

11 · 9 / 19

PostgreSQL versus CUBRID: benefits and costs

These are consequences of the structures at the pinned revisions, not measured speed comparisons.

Reuse bookkeeping
Advantage: shared usage credit avoids maintaining a recency-linked list for ordinary buffers. Cost: bounded credit loses fine ordering and repeated hand passes may be needed. CUBRID keeps domain/zone order, but it also avoids a list move for ordinary LRU1 reuse; it is not exact LRU on every hit.
A large scan beside H1/H2
Advantage: an eligible bulk operation can recycle a ring's frames, directly limiting its footprint. Cost: protection depends on the operation choosing the strategy and frames being reusable. CUBRID's domains and quotas address how groups compete across accesses, with more domain policy to maintain.
Finding reusable space
Advantage: the clock searches the shared descriptor pool without first choosing a quota domain. Cost: it may revisit many credited descriptors, and allocation can include dirty-page I/O. CUBRID bounds one selected-list LRU3 scan, but retries and cleaning can still delay allocation. Neither bound is a latency guarantee.

A ring hit only completes selection

Advance the local slot; empty, pinned or high-credit entries fall back to the global clock and replace that slot with the selected ID. BAS_NORMAL has no ring. BULKREAD, BULKWRITE and VACUUM have source-selected sizes; bulk-read sizing includes I/O concurrency expansion and caps, so “always 256 KiB” is not valid at this pin.

The selected descriptor already has this backend's pin. A dirty victim needs a conditional share-exclusive content lock. Failure releases the pin and retries, avoiding a possible wait cycle described in the source. A permanent BULKREAD ring victim needing WAL flush may be rejected and its slot invalidated. Otherwise FlushBuffer observes the page LSN and flushes WAL before the permanent-page write.

Three writers discharge different obligations · continued

Successful writes and OS writeback scheduling are distinct from durable storage. The final InvalidateVictimBuffer check requires refcount exactly one and no BM_DIRTY under mapping/header locks. A new user can dirty R and leave before this check, restoring refcount one while retaining debt.

Defend this responsibility

A's write succeeded; B later re-dirtied and unpinned R. Is refcount=1 sufficient for retirement?

Reveal the retirement argument

No. Current BM_DIRTY also rejects reuse. A valid optimization must exclude intervening dirties or preserve their image through another protocol. A coherent replacement: Reserve identity through writeback and retirement

Teaching alternative. Preserve the stated property while changing how actors coordinate.

  1. Install a reservation that lookup/pin paths must consult.
  2. Exclude writers, capture the matching log boundary and satisfy WAL.
  3. Write the stable bytes; retire the mapping while sole ownership and cleanliness still hold under mapping protection.
  4. On error or cancellation, keep the old identity valid with pending debt and remove the reservation before releasing its owner.

Teaching alternative: excludes intervening use instead of optimistically rechecking it. All lookup/content paths must obey the reservation and lock order; storage latency blocks readers/writers and checkpoint interactions need explicit ordering. The implemented path retains concurrency and may abandon reuse after writeback.

What observation could refute this explanation?

Hypothesis
A new DIRTY after writeback forces protected retirement to reject, even when refcount returns to one.
Controlled change
Pause after FlushBuffer content unlock; B pins, logs a mutation and unpins before A’s final invalidation. Keep permanent-page WAL assumptions explicit.
Exact observation
Proposed probe records LSN, content-lock outcome, post-write DIRTY/refcount, mapping locks and InvalidateVictimBuffer result. IOOP_REUSE/IOOP_EVICT is counted before final invalidation can reject.
Falsifier and limit
Retiring the old mapping with new DIRTY violates pending-work preservation. A counted eviction is not proof of successful final mapping retirement; checkpoint durability needs later sync/log evidence.
