From a Clock visit to claiming a candidate

The scan needs a candidate from the descriptor array. The atomic hand distributes visits; a visit may skip an owner, decrement credit, or attempt a claim. A successfully observed zero is not a pin: the state transition must succeed. Separate a quiet-pool visit proof from a concurrent progress claim.

A successful claim CAS establishes the new pin. A successful credit decrement resets trycounter. Credit saturation bounds stored credit, not concurrent replenishment, CAS interference, latency or starvation.

These are separate steps over time, not one atomic operation. In the 1 → 2 → 3 example, each access starts after that backend has fully released its previous pin, using the default strategy. Nested pins held by the same backend do not keep adding shared credit. A positive count buys another chance; it is neither an exact access count nor a timestamp. Aging happens when the hand visits, not after a fixed number of seconds.

Why this helps: repeated H1/H2 use can earn more chances than a one-time S page. What it loses: two pages with credit 2 have no recorded “more recent” ordering. The hand's position helps decide which is reached first.

09 · 6 / 18

PostgreSQL: the clock reduces reuse credits

PostgreSQL remembers bounded reuse credit, aged by clock visits rather than a recency list. The credit example shows the transitions; the source checklist follows.

  1. Admission: a newly claimed shared descriptor receives usage_count = 1.
  2. Reuse: the first pin by a backend increments shared usage_count up to 5. A nested pin by the same backend changes only its private refcount, so it earns no second shared credit.
  3. Victim: the atomic clock hand advances through buffer descriptors. It skips pinned buffers, decrements an unpinned positive count, and claims an unpinned zero. A count of 5 may therefore survive up to six full visits.
  4. Bulk scans: backend-local strategy rings reuse a small set of IDs for bulk read/write and vacuum. They still name frames in the shared pool; they are not private buffer pools.
No LRU order: two descriptors with usage_count = 2 have no “older one first” relation. The count is bounded reuse credit decayed by clock visits, not a timestamp or linked-list rank.

Q18 · A CAS decides the claim

Constructed starting state: A hand visit decrements D from credit 1 to 0 and moves on. At a later visit A reads refcount=0, credit=0. Before A’s expected-state CAS adds its pin, B succeeds at a normal first pin and retains it. No other change occurs.

Prediction: Which event determines whether A can return D?

Discuss, then reveal

A’s CAS against the old state fails. It rereads the state and sees B’s pin, so it cannot return D from this visit. Reading zero is an observation; successful CAS acquires protection. Even without interference, the positive-credit decrement visit moves on and a later zero-credit visit is needed for a claim.

09 · 9 / 18

A decrement is a visit; a successful claim CAS is a pin

On a backend's first normal pin, the pinned source increases shared refcount and usage_count up to five. Same-backend nested pins increase private count only. A strategy pin raises zero credit to one and preserves a higher existing value. Repeated nested calls do not give unlimited shared credit.

The clock advances an atomic hand, skips nonzero refcount, decrements positive credit by CAS, and continues to a later visit. For unpinned zero-credit state it attempts a CAS adding its pin, then records ownership. A failed CAS retries against the changed state; an observation of zero is not a claim.

Verified mechanism / Implementation policy: bufmgr.c:3295–3386 · freelist.c:239–317 · packed state and maximum credit. This revision uses 64-bit packed state, including content-lock state; older spinlock summaries are not the executable authority.

09 · 10 / 18

A global hand does not mean one allocator at a time

Each global sweep visit atomically advances nextVictimBuffer. Concurrent allocators can obtain different descriptor positions and inspect them concurrently. Wrap/pass bookkeeping uses a short spinlock; candidate aging and claiming use per-descriptor atomic state.

Constructed race: A observes an unpinned, zero-credit D. Before A claims it, B pins D. A’s compare-and-swap fails because D’s state changed; A retries and sees the pin. An earlier observation cannot authorize reuse.

Cost hypothesis: Clock avoids recency-list splices on ordinary hits, but many misses can contend on the hand and revisit credited pages. Many accesses to one page can contend on its state. CUBRID distributes list work across domains but also synchronizes BCBs, lists and quota state. Measure these costs under a stated workload.

Exact recency is a different synchronization budget

Teaching alternative: Maintain a shared doubly linked exact-LRU order and move a frame on each chosen access. A known-node splice is O(1) pointer work, but concurrent hits need list synchronization and metadata writes. Clock instead uses bounded credit updates and defers aging to allocation visits; it approximates retention and shares an atomic hand/state.

A FIFO or zero-credit clock can still respect pins/content/WAL rules while changing victim quality. Removing only credit is a policy change, not inherently corruption. Counting every nested pin in shared state also preserves lifetime if every release matches, but adds shared atomics and changes the meaning of the count.

For V visited descriptors and R failed CAS attempts, selection has O(V + R) state operations, excluding header-lock waits, content waits and writeback. Descriptor policy state is O(N) for N buffers; a backend adds private use state. Source does not tell us which alternative is faster under a given contention pattern.

A finite credit cap is not a concurrent progress theorem

Under a single allocator, stable pin membership, at least one permanently unpinned frame, no new credit and successful state operations, a credit cap U gives a loose bound of N(U+1) visits to find a zero-credit frame. This is a teaching bound on visits, not elapsed time. With U=5, a quiet static pool cannot retain positive credit indefinitely.

Derivation (Inference under the stated model): choose one permanently unpinned frame j. Every complete sweep of N visits reaches j. If its credit is positive, a successful decrement reduces it by one and resets trycounter. Starting at credit at most U, at most U such sweeps exhaust that credit. The next visit claims j, giving the loose N(U+1) bound; an earlier claim only shortens the search. Visiting j also interrupts any run of N pinned observations, so the pinned-only error cannot preempt this proof. Replenishment or failed state operations breaks its monotonic premise.

Concurrent backends can add credit, pin candidates, change the hand and interfere with CAS. The trycounter starts at N but resets on successful decrement; N is not a total scan cap. A sequence of pinned visits without intervening progress can instead report “no unpinned buffers available.” This does not prove permanent exhaustion across all future schedules.

atomic hand · retry/reset/error branches. Neither global allocator fairness nor a maximum latency is established.

D29 · Does usage_count≤5 bound one allocation to six passes?

In a four-descriptor teaching pool, all frames are initially unpinned and credit≤5. Another backend repeatedly pins a candidate and renews its credit before the allocator's claim. Can the quiet-pool bound be reused unchanged?

Challenge, then reveal the bounded answer

No. The proof's no-new-credit and stable-pin premises fail. Saturation bounds the instantaneous counter, not the number of replenishments or CAS failures. State visits, successful decrements and successful claims must be counted separately.

Follow-up: “An exact LRU avoids this?” It still needs lifetime protection and a rule for concurrent usage. Replacing the policy does not discharge reuse safety or give scheduler fairness.

Discriminating observation: Collect hand tickets, observed pins/credit, successful decrement/claim CAS and interference retries. Hold workload and pin duration constant when comparing policies. Preserve Q18–Q20 and both existing fresh clock traces.

A coherent replacement: Exact recency under a shared order

Teaching alternative. Preserve the stated property while changing how actors coordinate.

  1. Define which successful access event changes recency.
  2. Serialize every such event into one protected MRU/LRU order.
  3. Scan oldest candidates while retaining independent pin/dirty/mapping checks.
  4. After interference or write failure, preserve identity and debt, update order consistently, then retry.

Teaching alternative: O(n) resident-order metadata for n frames, O(1) list mutation per access with shared synchronization, and up to O(n) eligibility scans. History-aware alternatives add nonresident identity history. Clock avoids exact global recency maintenance, but CAS and revisits still cost work; exact order alone does not establish fairness.
