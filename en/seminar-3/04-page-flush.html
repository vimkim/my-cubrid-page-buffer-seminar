What remains blocked?

The page-flush producer remembers P while holding a list lock. That lock is released before the producer later locks the BCB. Stable BCB storage does not freeze VPID, use, hotness or dirty state. 04 · 3 / 17

Timer and frame pressure wake page-flush

The daemon runs periodically and can be woken by frame pressure. Waking does not establish that a page was written; the task checks waiting allocators and current replacement pressure.

pgbuf-page-flush pauses for page_bg_flush_interval, 1,000 ms by default. A value of zero means infinite wait until a wake. Allocation/victim pressure wakes it when normal victim search fails, when removing a clean bottom exposes a dirty bottom, or when a selected victim scan finds no reusable BCB.

After the boot gate passes, a wake reported by the waiter forces at least one pgbuf_flush_victim_candidates() call. A timer timeout calls it only while a direct-victim waiter exists or the sampled hit ratio is below 99.9% after enough victim requests. One task execution may loop through several calls while pressure remains; a timed pass can also submit zero pages.

04 · 5 / 17

Collect candidates, then check current state and WAL

  1. Read pressure and compute per-LRU priority plus a scan budget.
  2. Take one LRU mutex at a time, walk cold LRU3 nodes, and save candidate BCB pointer plus VPID.
  3. Release LRU locks and optionally sort candidates by VPID for sequentiality.
  4. For each candidate, lock its BCB and recheck the same VPID, DIRTY, no current FLUSHING, still LRU3, and zero fixes.
  5. If its WAL boundary is not ready, skip it and wake log-flush. Under allocator pressure, the code can force the needed log boundary once and retry.
  6. Copy and submit one stable generation through DWB or direct I/O, possibly with neighboring pages.

For the 512 MiB/16 KiB example, B=32,768. The default 1% unboosted selection budget is 327 checks; the maximum 10× boost is 3,270, below the 12,800-page 200 MiB cap. These are inspection budgets, not write counts.

04 · 8 / 17

Remembered candidates are observations across a gap

The BCB array supplies stable management-object storage, but its current VPID and state can change. A candidate record saves both BCB pointer and VPID: the pointer locates the object; identity detects F/P becoming F/Q. A matching VPID cannot prove that P stayed unfixed, cold or free of another flush.

Safety: authorize capture or reuse from current protected state, and never reuse while another caller owns a fix or unpreserved dirty work. Policy: prefer LRU3 and respect the private quota guard. Identity and fixes protect different facts; coldness is not a durability certificate.

Inference: Collecting under the list mutex then releasing it before BCB work bounds list protection. It creates a deliberate validation gap, not a reservation.

04 · 16 / 17

A coherent replacement: Reserve a candidate across the gap

Teaching alternative. Preserve the stated property while changing how actors coordinate.

  1. Under list and descriptor protection, install a reservation recognized by every competing reuse path.
  2. Remember the reserved identity; release list protection but retain the reservation.
  3. Acquire byte protection and capture the image under the reservation protocol.
  4. On cancellation or flush failure, restore pending debt and remove the reservation before releasing its lifetime reference.

Teaching alternative: identity stays reserved instead of being merely observed. All fix/reuse paths must respect the new state; longer exclusion can impede hot accesses. Sorting requires extra candidate storage and O(c log c) comparisons for c collected candidates. The implemented path instead pays current rechecks.

What observation could refute this explanation?

Unexecuted experiment proposal. Record the full pinned revision, build, pool/log/DWB/I/O settings and workload before interpreting a result.

Hypothesis
Current rechecks distinguish stale nominations from useful cleaning supply.
Controlled change
Hold the list-unlock→BCB-lock gap and independently change VPID, fcnt or DIRTY. Restore the same initial checkpoint for each change.
Exact observation
Built-in skip counters combine several rejection causes. Proposed per-candidate probe records saved/current VPID, the exact rejecting branch and captured LSA versus durable WAL.
Falsifier and limit
Wrong candidate attribution after removing VPID is the bounded counterexample; it does not alone establish corrupted bytes. Observer-held locks must not create the gap being measured.
