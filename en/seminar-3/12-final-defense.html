Protection follows ownership across asynchronous boundaries

  1. Acquire identity/lifetime protection for use; content access follows its own rules.
  2. Mutation creates log and propagation obligations; release repays use debt only.
  3. Capture/submit an image under its WAL rule; image completion does not retire future mutations.
  4. Recheck current state when selecting, consuming, receiving or invalidating across a protection gap.
  5. Retire the old mapping and materialize the requested identity only through the protected acquisition protocol.

Alternative protocols need explicit receipts and costs

Teaching alternative: Transfer typed receipts for “image G accepted,” “G bookkeeping completed,” “current F reserved” and “old mapping retired.” Each receipt names its identity/generation/protected interval and cancellation rule. A reservation may exclude new users; an image receipt must never be promoted into lifetime ownership.

Continuous reservation spends concurrency and requires abandonment cleanup. Revalidation spends metadata reads, synchronization and possible retry. Foreground completion shifts CPU/I/O waiting to the requesting caller; background work shifts it to another actor and introduces queue/lifecycle dependencies.

Sum useful scan, image-copy, queue and state-transition work separately from lock, WAL, storage and scheduler delay. An upper bound on structural work under a quiet schedule is not a bound on concurrent end-to-end latency. Any throughput claim needs a controlled workload, storage configuration, pin durations and repeated measurements.

A coherent replacement: Typed image receipts and separate current claims

Teaching alternative. Preserve the stated property while changing how actors coordinate.

  1. Name image identity and the exact accepted/written/durable boundary in each receipt.
  2. Retire only the named outstanding image.
  3. Make a fresh protected claim for current reuse; reject any new use or dirty obligation.
  4. On failure, retry the appropriate obligation instead of promoting an old receipt to present authority.

Teaching alternative: explicit ticket/version lifetime and cancellation ownership add metadata. Continuous serialization is another coherent choice with long exclusion. Any replacement must preserve WAL, image consistency, new debt, identity lifetime and a discoverable progress path together.

Close the journey: one request, a reusable frame, another identity

  1. A caller finds the page needed for a record operation. One operation may visit several pages.
  2. The buffer resolves the page identity to a frame and BCB, loading on a miss. Fix protects its use lifetime; a compatible latch grants access to its bytes.
  3. Matching unfixes repay use debt. Ordinary final unfix can update admission or reuse policy; it does not promise eviction.
  4. Replacement chooses a domain and candidate, then rechecks current safety under protection. Dirty data must be preserved, and background work can help without guaranteeing an immediately reusable frame.
  5. Only after safe detachment and old mapping removal can the same frame carry a new page identity. Its stable address does not preserve the old identity.

H1/H2 survival depends on the established reuse schedule and policy state. The comparison showed other ways to remember reuse, with different costs; it established no speed ranking.

Constructed starting state: A constructed module documents Acquire(P) → Use → Release(P) and SubmitImage(P) → Complete(ticket) → Offer(frame) → Retag(frame,Q). Prediction: Which transition contracts must be defined before explaining safe reuse?
