Which buffers can a large scan reuse?

The large scan should not earn unlimited retention merely by touching many pages once. Its access-strategy ring remembers a bounded set of shared buffer IDs. Those IDs give a reuse opportunity, not exclusive frames. NORMAL, BULKREAD, BULKWRITE and VACUUM choose different policy and I/O assumptions.

A backend-local ring owns its ID array, not shared frame contents. Shared refcount and credit rejection must be respected even if another ring names the same frame; BULKREAD WAL rejection invalidates that local slot.

PostgreSQL: let a bulk scan reuse a small ring

The clock alone does not recognize every scan. A bulk operation can use an access-strategy ring: a small backend-local array containing IDs of frames in the shared pool. The operation cycles through those slots and tries to reuse their frames.

Q19 · Two rings, one frame ID

Constructed starting state: Backend A and B have ring slots containing shared buffer ID F. F initially represents R. A performs the protected identity change to S and releases its pin. B’s array is unchanged. B visits F, now clean, refcount=0, credit=1.

Prediction: What does B’s unchanged slot say about the current identity?

Discuss, then reveal

It still refers to F, which now represents S. The slot does not own or preserve R. B can attempt the current frame’s protected ring claim, then perform the required identity/writeback work. Compare a backend-local reference array with CUBRID’s pool-owned BCB policy membership, rather than equating private storage ownership.

Q20 · Same candidate, different strategy

Constructed starting state: Three independent runs use BULKREAD, BULKWRITE and VACUUM rings. Each selects an unpinned credit-1 dirty BM_PERMANENT page from its ring, obtains the required content lock without waiting, and needs a WAL flush through the page LSN. No other backend intervenes.

Prediction: How can the three strategies differ after selection?

Discuss, then reveal

BULKREAD rejects this ring candidate, clears its current slot to InvalidBuffer and releases lock/pin before retry. This branch does not invalidate the shared page identity. BULKWRITE and VACUUM do not use that bulk-read rejection: they can force WAL and flush before protected invalidation. NORMAL has no strategy ring. Factory size is policy; caller size, AIO/pin limits and the NBuffers/8 cap determine actual sizes at this pin.

10 · 8 / 13

A scan needs space without earning unlimited retention

A one-pass scan can bring many pages through a shared pool while reusing few of them. If each visit earns normal clock credit, the scan may displace a repeatedly used working set. Strategy rings bound the scan's preferred reuse footprint using backend-local shared-buffer IDs, not a separately allocated private cache.

Policy: Try local ring slots with shared refcount=0 and credit≤1; higher credit suggests other use. Safety: Claim through shared state, preserve dirty data/WAL, then retire the old mapping under protected current-state checks.

Two local rings can name F4. Neither owns F4 permanently. Successful pin CAS by one makes the other's slot temporarily unusable. An ID can later name a different page in the same shared frame.

10 · 9 / 13

A local footprint and a local pool are different alternatives

No ring: Keep the normal clock and all safety checks. Scan traffic now uses normal allocation/credit paths; pollution and metadata traffic may increase, but correctness can remain intact. Teaching alternative: Allocate a separate private scan pool. This isolates capacity but duplicates cached bytes and needs coherence if writers and normal readers access the same pages. A shared admission policy could instead deprioritize one-touch pages, adding classification/history state.

Ring memory is O(r) IDs per strategy object of r slots; a slot attempt does O(1) local work plus shared-state CAS retries. Fallback adds clock visits. It reserves no r exclusive shared frames. Avoiding high-credit IDs is a policy choice; bypassing pinned checks is a lifetime error.

An alternative that eliminates backend eviction writes needs guaranteed clean supply or explicit wait/failure when none exists. Moving I/O to another actor does not eliminate it.

10 · 10 / 13

Defend this responsibility

Two rings contain F4, now unpinned with credit 2. Can both reuse it because their IDs are local?

Reveal the ring argument

No. Local IDs confer no ownership and current credit>1 rejects both ring attempts. With credit≤1, a successful claim by one still changes shared refcount; the other must re-evaluate. Removing only the credit gate changes retention policy; removing the pin gate threatens lifetime.

Follow-up: BAS_BULKREAD WAL rejection invalidates its own current slot, preventing repeated selection of the same costly dirty ring entry. It neither clears the shared dirty page nor changes every strategy.

10 · 12 / 13

A coherent replacement: Partition scan capacity under one identity map

Teaching alternative. Preserve the stated property while changing how actors coordinate.

  1. Reserve a bounded frame partition for scan admission.
  2. Use one authoritative identity map across partitions.
  3. Respect shared use and dirty obligations before migration or reuse.
  4. On scan exit, release its reservation and make idle capacity available under an explicit rebalance policy.

Teaching alternative: reserves actual capacity, unlike a ring of r local IDs. It adds rebalance/coherence rules and may strand idle space. A 2Q/history policy is a different scan-resistance alternative with queues/history. Ring local work is O(1) plus shared-state retry; IDs use O(r) memory but do not bound all scan I/O or guarantee r exclusive frames.

What observation could refute this explanation?

Unexecuted experiment proposal. Record the full pinned revision, build, pool/log/DWB/I/O settings and workload before interpreting a result.

Hypothesis
Scan rings reduce a local reuse footprint only under their eligibility and sizing assumptions.
Controlled change
Keep the hot-set/scan request sequence fixed; compare NORMAL and each supported strategy with stated pool, I/O and ring settings. Add two rings naming one frame as a separate schedule.
Exact observation
pg_stat_io exposes strategy contexts. Proposed probes separate ring accept, refcount/credit rejection, BULKREAD WAL rejection and global fallback; record slots versus distinct shared frames.
Falsifier and limit
A ring ID authorizing use despite another owner refutes lifetime protection. r slots do not prove r exclusive frames or an r-page I/O bound; aggregate reuse counts cannot establish final retirement.
