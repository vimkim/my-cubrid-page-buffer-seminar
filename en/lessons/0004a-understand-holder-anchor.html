Separate the holder anchor from a holder entry

By the end, you can show exactly how one thread recognizes a repeated fix of BCB A, why no list position is remembered, and when a holder entry moves between active and free lists.

01 · One sentence first

The anchor owns lists; an entry records one thread–BCB relationship

PGBUF_HOLDER_ANCHOR is one thread's small directory. A PGBUF_HOLDER is one reusable record saying “this thread currently owes N unfixes to this BCB.”

The anchor does not represent a page. It gives one thread access to its active and free holder-entry lists.

ObjectCardinality and fieldsMeaning
PGBUF_HOLDER_ANCHOROne per thread index: thrd_hold_list, thrd_free_list, num_hold_cnt, num_free_cnt.Routes that thread to active and reusable entries. It has no BCB pointer and no nested-fix count.
PGBUF_HOLDERZero or more per thread: bufptr, fix_count, thrd_link/next_holder, statistics, watchers.When active, binds this thread to one BCB and counts this thread's nested debts for it.
02 · Read the snapshot

Two active entries can represent four successful fixes

In the diagram, Thread 17 has fixed BCB X three times and BCB Y once. Its anchor therefore has num_hold_cnt = 2, because it owns two distinct active entries. The sum of their nested debts is 3 + 1 = 4.

Do not equate the counters: num_hold_cnt is the active-list length; it is not the sum of holder fix_count values.

Thread 18 can also fix BCB X. That produces a separate holder under Thread 18's separate anchor. BCB X's global fcnt combines grants from both threads, while each holder stores only its own thread's share.

03 · Answer the lookup question

It scans by BCB address; it does not remember a position

For another fix of BCB A, pgbuf_find_thrd_holder(thread_p, bufptr_A) walks the current thread's active list and compares holder->bufptr == bufptr_A.

  1. Resolve the current thread's anchor. thread_p->m_holder_anchor lazily caches &pgbuf_Pool.thrd_holder_info[thread_p->index].
  2. Start at m_holder_anchor->thrd_hold_list.
  3. Compare each active entry's bufptr with BCB A's pointer.
  4. Follow thrd_link until a match or NULL.
  5. On a match, a successful repeated grant increments that same entry's fix_count. On no match, a successful first grant obtains a free entry, prepends it to the active list, stores BCB A in bufptr, and sets fix_count = 1.
CaseHolder resultAccounting result after grant

What is absent: there is no remembered numeric position, per-thread holder hash table, or BCB-owned reverse list of holder threads in this path. The lookup is linear in the number of distinct BCBs currently held by that thread.

Verified mechanism: the exact traversal is pinned page_buffer.c:6090–6126. Normal fix looks up at 6319, increments a match at 6494–6510, and allocates a first entry at 6511–6531. The resident READ fast path repeats the match-or-allocate decision at 7753–7782.

Allocation pops the free-list head and prepends it to the hold-list head. This constant-time insertion explains why a holder has no stable ordinal position: later first-time fixes can put new entries before it.

05 · Storage lifetime versus binding lifetime

Final unfix removes the binding, not the backing allocation

The entry is recycled; one logical thread–BCB binding is temporary.

Initialization gives every thread seven reserved free entries. If a thread exhausts its free list, allocation obtains an entry from shared extension sets of ten. A first successful fix activates one entry. Each repeated successful fix increments it. Each matching unfix decrements it; when it reaches zero, removal unlinks it from the active list and pushes it onto that thread's free list. Watchers must already be detached.

Verified mechanism: initialization and allocation are at page_buffer.c:5922–6086; final-debt removal and recycling are at 6128–6275. Backing holder storage is reclaimed during page-buffer finalization, not by an ordinary unfix.

06 · Unfix and cost

Release finds the same entry by another linear scan

pgbuf_unlatch_thrd_holder() calls the same lookup, decrements the matching entry, and removes it at zero. Removal of a non-head entry must find its predecessor in the singly linked active list, so a final unfix can traverse the list again.

Performance inference: one thread holding many distinct BCBs can pay linear lookup costs, and an unfavorable series of removals can accumulate quadratic list work. Repeated fixes of one BCB reuse one entry and do not lengthen the list. Many threads fixing one BCB create a different hypothesis: contention on that BCB's shared atomic latch. Measure these separately before calling either one the bottleneck.

Explain A×3 without saying “the anchor finds its position”

Prompt
