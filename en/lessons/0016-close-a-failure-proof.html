01 · Retrieve the evidence boundary

Five claims require five kinds of evidence

  1. Source-visible control flow: pinned text shows a suspicious ordering or exit.
  2. Reachability: a supported target executes that exact branch.
  3. Surviving state: debt remains after callee cleanup, scope cleanup, wakeups, and retry.
  4. Production impact: a consumer observes incorrectness, leak, hang, corruption, or unacceptable progress.
  5. Current-branch status: the same path and effect are re-established on the revision being changed.

A proof may stop at any level. Its conclusion must stop there too.

02 · When to inspect

Include deferred cleanup in the final-state proof

A fallible callee may clean its own resources. A scope guard or outer caller may release a mutex, remove a load record, restore flags, wake waiters, or transform the error into retry. Therefore, record state at two points:

  1. At the exact return seam, to prove the branch and local ordering.
  2. After the complete owner protocol settles, to prove what survives and whether retry can make progress.
A low-level mock that never constructs the BCB flags, holder ledger, load ownership, or waiter state cannot prove their cleanup.

06 · Proof receipt

Record the experiment's evidence boundary

  1. Exact source revision, build mode, configuration, and owner path.
  2. Injection or schedule and proof that the target branch executed.
  3. Before/after ledger values after the complete unwind.
  4. Retry, waiter, liveness, victim, checkpoint, or restart consequence.
  5. Supported conclusion and explicitly unsupported conclusion.
  6. Reproducer receipt and current registry ID; update status only in the registry.

VS-13 is an error-visibility question, while VS-14 is a controlled identity/reuse and memory-order proof obligation. Do not force every uncertainty into the same fault-injection shape.

Upgrade a weak “stress passed” conclusion
