Lecture F1 — Replacement foundations

Start with basic programming and arrays/linked lists. Predict what leaves when there is no free slot; no prior buffer-pool or WAL knowledge is required.

The cache is full. T arrives. Who leaves?

Three-hour route ↑

Three fast slots hold P, R and S. To bring in T, one resident page must give up its slot. The choice matters when we need that page again.

For this example: fixed capacity, equal-sized pages, serial requests, a valid backing copy, and every resident page immediately reusable. Every miss loads its page; growth and bypass are disabled.

A page is data. A frame is its reusable slot.

A page is a named, fixed-size block of data. A frame is a reusable slot that holds one page. Page P can leave frame F0; F0 still exists and can hold T. A hit finds the requested page resident. A miss requires obtaining it from the backing store in this model.

Temporal locality means reusing the same data soon. Spatial locality means using nearby data; bringing a block may serve later nearby accesses. Our recurring story uses P and R repeatedly, interrupted by a scan of distinct pages S, T, U. P/R form the small working set: the pages repeatedly needed in this phase. Reuse is a workload property, not a promise that a policy will retain P and R.

Cache layers and an illustrative cost

A cache keeps a subset of data in storage that is faster to access. Registers, CPU caches, main memory, and persistent storage form a hierarchy with different capacity and access costs; the exact costs depend on the machine. Here we isolate one fast cache and a slower backing store.

For an invented serial cost example, lookup costs 1 unit and a miss adds 100 units. Ten requests with three misses cost 10 × 1 + 3 × 100 = 310 units. Hit counts alone omit policy bookkeeping, overlapping I/O, and contention; these units are not hardware measurements.

Trace conventions

Each policy starts empty and follows the same ten requests. P/R recur; S/T/U are scan pages.

F0, F1, F2 are stable physical slots. Initially all are empty (—). Fill the lowest-numbered empty frame first; on replacement, load into the victim's frame. A row shows state after its request. H = hit, M = miss; victim — means no eviction. All ten requests, including initial fills, count toward totals. Queue order is metadata, not physical frame movement.

This is an authored teaching model, not a CUBRID configuration or runtime observation. Equal-sized pages, serial requests, uniform miss costs, and unrestricted victim eligibility keep the policy question separate from database safety.

FIFO: remember arrival, ignore hits

FIFO evicts the earliest-loaded resident page. Its queue runs oldest → newest; hits leave it unchanged. Empty queue initially; serial admissions cannot tie. At request 7, T needs an occupied frame.

At request 7, FIFO evicts P: the hits did not change its arrival order.

Exact LRU: remember every access

LRU remembers every access, including hits. At request 3, P stays in F0 but becomes most recent.

Clock trades exact order for one bit

Frames form a ring F0 → F1 → F2 → F0. Initially the hand points at F0 and bits are 000. Every hit sets that frame's bit to 1 and does not move the hand. An empty-frame load sets its bit to 1 and moves the hand to the following frame.

On a full-cache miss, inspect the hand's frame: clear a 1 bit and advance; at a 0 bit, evict and load, set its bit to 1, then advance once. Metadata is bits in F0/F1/F2 order; next hand. Predict request 7 from 111; F0. Which bits get cleared?

A 1 bit buys another inspection, not permanent protection. Clear the bit and advance; replace at the first 0.

One bit records use since the last clearing, not the exact order of uses; Clock approximates recency and a miss can inspect several frames.

Five distinct pages require at least five misses from empty, so this run reaches that lower bound. OPT is an offline comparison under these assumptions; a live system generally cannot know the entire future.

A known workload is not a known future

Known trace → calculate OPT. With the exact future page sequence, OPT minimizes misses in this model. A recorded trace lets us calculate that ideal offline; OPT does not predict the future.

Known workload pattern → tune and measure. A configured sysbench workload can guide policy tuning. Knowing its access distribution does not by itself reveal the exact future page sequence or guarantee an optimal policy.

Unknown future → estimate reuse. Perfect future knowledge is generally unavailable. Practical policies use available information to estimate what will be useful again. Some deterministic sequences are predictable.

Counterexample, Random and LFU

Separate counterexample, reset to empty: P R S P T R. With three frames, FIFO hits P and the final R (2 hits, 4 misses); LRU hits only P (1 hit, 5 misses). Before T, FIFO's oldest page is P, but LRU's oldest is R. Thus recent use does not guarantee future use, and exact LRU need not beat FIFO.

Random chooses a resident victim randomly, avoiding recency ordering but risking a hot page. A particular random outcome is not an expected performance result. LFU prefers the least frequently used page; it needs count, tie, and aging rules. Without aging, an old popular page can remain favored after the workload changes. Neither is a universal winner. Compare misses, bookkeeping, and the actual workload.

Reveal explanation and state

FIFO and Clock hit only request 4: 1 hit, 6 misses. LRU hits 4 and 7: 2 hits, 5 misses. OPT hits 4, 6, and 7: 3 hits, 4 misses. At request 5 both FIFO and LRU evict P. What carries forward

Keep the workload shape: repeated P/R requests interrupted by S/T/U scan traffic. Keep the distinction between a page identity and a reusable frame, and between policy metadata and page contents. The database bridge adds in-use and dirty-page constraints before applying this reasoning to real buffer pools.

The main trace's final physical frames are FIFO/Clock: R / U / P; OPT: P / R / U; LRU: U / P / R. These are four alternative histories, not one shared state. The later CUBRID worked example starts from its own source-derived larger-pool snapshot. Only the workload idea transfers; neither three-frame arithmetic nor exact-LRU movement is asserted for CUBRID.
