LECTURE 12 / Replacement

Replacement state and progress

One permanent BCB/frame pair moves through admission, cooling, victim checks, and rebinding to another identity.

INVALID firstVOID is unlinkedLRU3 onlyProtected recheck

A page miss needs a frame; where does that frame come from?

If an unused BCB/frame pair exists on the INVALID list, CUBRID pops it. No resident page is removed. Only after INVALID is empty does CUBRID search the cold end of an LRU for a resident page that is safe to replace.

Replacement changes the VPID and bytes inside a fixed BCB/frame pair. It does not manufacture a new pair and does not swap BCB A to another frame.

The short route is: INVALID pop → load in VOID → final unfix places the BCB in an LRU → list pressure demotes it toward LRU3 → a clean idle LRU3 BCB may be detached → its old hash identity is removed → the same pair is rebound.

Why CUBRID's LRU is not textbook exact LRU

Exact LRU remembers the precise order of accesses. CUBRID uses conditional placement within several three-zone lists, not one exact global recency order. Both use past activity to guide replacement, but they do not promise the same next victim.

Recall the textbook model: every hit or admission makes that page the most recently used (MRU). On a full-cache miss, the least recently used resident page leaves. That model assumes every resident page is immediately eligible. A database must additionally enforce ownership and durability constraints, regardless of its replacement policy.

Think of exact LRU as an access-ordered line. CUBRID instead organizes resident members into several lines, each split into protected zones LRU1/LRU2 and the searchable zone LRU3. Updating those lines is conditional. These are policy regions, not three physical buffer pools; moving a BCB changes metadata, not the location of its page bytes.

Final unfix is a decision point, not an automatic move

Final means the global fix count reaches zero, not merely that one caller has finished. If another fix remains, this zero-count placement path is not reached. A move-to-bottom request and reader/writer waiter handoff also affect whether the ordinary zone branch runs.

Within that ordinary branch, ignore hints and private-to-shared migration are checked before the usual zone result:

  • LRU1: keep position when those exceptions do not apply.
  • LRU2: boost to LRU1 only when the list-event age is old enough; otherwise keep position.
  • LRU3: normally boost to LRU1 after the exception checks.

Cooling goes the other way. A scan admits more pages; when zone thresholds are exceeded, boundary nodes can move LRU1 → LRU2 → LRU3 even without another access to that page. Wall-clock time alone does not perform this movement. The existing scan example derives the exact boundary changes.

Do not combine three different notions of age: list-event age controls the LRU2 boost test; the accepted quota-adjustment epoch limits activity sampling; a separate hot-fix heuristic contributes to migration. None is a precise timestamp of the last access, and LRU1 membership does not prove that the separate hotness test is true.

Three decisions: placement, search, safe reuse

  1. Placement: which list and zone should hold this BCB? A private list is a policy domain associated with execution contexts, not an exclusive private copy. Multiple contexts can share a domain. A domain mismatch or hot-and-old condition can move a private member to shared LRU2 rather than its current LRU1 top.
  2. Search: when INVALID is exhausted, which list should supply a candidate? Quota and advertised candidates influence own-private, other-private and shared search. Quota is a policy target, not a fixed partition of physical memory. Selection does not find a globally oldest page or reserve a frame by itself.
  3. Safe reuse: may this candidate's old identity actually be retired? The selected-list scan checks LRU3, flags, fixes, waiters and locking, then rechecks under protection. Detachment, old hash-identity removal and new binding are separate events.

Safety is not a CUBRID-only difference from LRU. A database choosing exact LRU would still need to protect pinned pages and preserve dirty data through its WAL/writeback protocol. A preferred page may be unusable; an eligible alternative may be chosen. Flush can leave the page resident, and re-dirty can leave it ineligible. Neither a good ranking nor waiting alone guarantees progress.

Why reduce ordering updates, and what costs remain?

The R/P example avoids the relink needed to maintain exact recency. With concurrent access, relinking shared metadata also requires coordination. The pinned source explicitly describes keeping an LRU1 member in place as a performance intention; that comment is not a benchmark result.

CUBRID still pays for BCB synchronization, conditional list updates, boundaries, activity/quota accounting and candidate scans. Multiple lists can distribute some metadata work but do not eliminate contention. Exact LRU and CUBRID can choose different victims, so reduced ordering work may trade against different miss behavior. The net effect depends on the workload and must be measured.

Do not conclude that CUBRID is always faster, scan-proof, optimal or starvation-free. At this baseline AOUT is dormant, so “CUBRID is 2Q” is also not an adequate description. Existing quota/search/progress uncertainties remain in the evidence registry; the explanation does not resolve them.

BCB A can age without a clock ticking it down

  1. Startup: A is an INVALID-list node paired permanently with frame A.
  2. First miss for page P: pgbuf_get_bcb_from_invalid_list() pops A. A becomes VOID, receives VPID P, loads P's bytes, and is fixed.
  3. First final unfix: when global fcnt reaches zero, the execution context's enabled private-LRU assignment selects private LRU1 top; without one, A enters shared LRU2 middle. Lecture 0012B owns the exact rule.
  4. Cooling: later insertions make protected zones exceed their thresholds. pgbuf_lru_adjust_zones() demotes old boundary nodes LRU1 → LRU2 → LRU3. Time alone does not move A.
  5. Second chance: if P is fixed again, a final unfix from LRU3 normally boosts A to LRU1. A must cool again.
  6. Victim: if A stays clean and idle in LRU3, a selected scan may lock and detach it to protected VOID.
  7. Rebind: pgbuf_victimize_bcb() rechecks A, removes P from the resident hash, and the miss path binds the same A/frame pair to page Q.

“Candidate,” “detached,” and “victimized” are different moments. LRU3 membership makes A searchable. Detach removes it from the LRU but old P is still hash-visible. Victimization removes P's identity. Only then can Q own the pair.

List membership is not transaction ownership

A session borrows one already-existing private-list ID; it does not receive a private cache. Multiple sessions may share the list, another domain may fix its BCBs, and releasing an assignment does not drain them.

CUBRID waits until a BCB becomes safe to reuse

If INVALID is empty and normal search finds nothing, pgbuf_allocate_bcb() can enter a direct-victim waiter queue and wake the flush daemon. A producer may clean and reserve a BCB. If another fix reaches it before consumption, the reservation is revoked and the allocator retries.

A DIRTY LRU3 BCB becomes reusable only after WAL-safe page write completion. During the write, FLUSHING still blocks replacement. If the page is dirtied again as generation G+1, that newer debt prevents direct assignment.

Every allocation path ends in assignment, retry, interrupt, timeout, or explicit error. ER_PB_ALL_BUFFERS_DIRTY is a fallback error name and does not prove that every BCB was literally dirty. The source does not prove wall-clock fairness for one waiter.

Source: page_buffer.c:8181–8403,9067–9253,10925–10952,15420–15652,16972–17255.

An unfixed page is still dirty

A BCB has zero fixes and belongs to LRU3, but it is DIRTY. Can the allocator reuse it immediately?

Explore the explanation

No. Zero fixes and LRU3 membership are necessary checks, not a complete safety proof. Dirty bytes must not be discarded. Victim selection also checks current flags, owners, waiters, and protected state; progress may require flushing and retrying before a safe frame exists.
