01 · The comparison rule

Every engine must answer two questions

1. May this frame be reused? A fix or pin says no while somebody owns it. 2. May another worker touch these bytes incompatibly? A latch or content lock says no. The names differ; the two safety jobs do not.

Do not map APIs by name. Map the safety responsibility and the cleanup owner.

02 · Three sentences to remember

How one worker borrows a page

CUBRID couples the normal fix and latch request. PostgreSQL exposes pin and content lock separately. InnoDB keeps the states separate but lets one MTR memo own their lifetime.

03 · Replacement in one line

All reject owned frames; they remember “hot” differently

EngineOne-line replacement picture
CUBRIDPages age through LRU1 → LRU2 → LRU3 across private/shared lists; victim search looks in LRU3.
PostgreSQLA clock hand skips pinned buffers, reduces positive usage_count, and chooses an unpinned zero.
InnoDBNew reads enter the old part of a young/old LRU; qualified reuse promotes them, and candidates come from the old tail.

04 · Boundary of the comparison

Benchmark claims require measured evidence

  • Clock sweep is not inherently faster than LRU, and midpoint LRU is not inherently better than either.
  • Replacement policy cannot be compared apart from pool size, access pattern, dirty-page pressure, sharding, and concurrency.
  • Copying another engine's data structure would also require preserving CUBRID's holder, waiter, flush-generation, quota, and direct-victim contracts.

Use the comparison to form a workload-specific question—not to declare a winner.
